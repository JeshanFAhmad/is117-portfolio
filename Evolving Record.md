The intended audience is people who prefer simple, understated clothing and want an uncomplicated everyday white T-shirt. The site should communicate the product clearly, make important information easy to find, and avoid unsupported claims.

My career research currently focuses on two possible directions:

## 3. Two Portfolio Positions

I considered two approaches for presenting my portfolio.

### Position A: Simple Informational Portfolio

This version prioritizes a short introduction, clear navigation, and a direct list of projects and professional links. It is intended for visitors who want to find information quickly.

**Strengths:** Simple to understand, quick to navigate, and less likely to distract from the work.

**Trade-offs:** It may feel plain and may not communicate the context or value of each project as effectively.

### Position B: Project-Focused Portfolio

This version gives projects more space through descriptions, previews, and links to repositories or live sites. It provides more context about what each project does and what I contributed.

**Strengths:** Gives visitors a better understanding of the work and makes project evidence more prominent.

**Trade-offs:** Requires more content and careful organization. If too much information is shown at once, the site could become harder to scan.

### Current Direction

My current direction is to use the project-focused approach while keeping the navigation and layout simple. This combines the clearer project explanations of Position B with the direct navigation of Position A.

This is a design decision, not a completed comparison test. I still need to make both versions available as working versions and have a classmate attempt the same task on each before treating the choice as verified.

## 4. Persona Hypotheses

These are assumptions to guide design and testing, not confirmed interview findings.

### Persona Hypothesis 1: Recruiter or Hiring Manager

A recruiter may have limited time and want to quickly understand who I am, what I can do, and where to see examples of my work.

## 6. Everyday Storefront: Brand and Design Decisions

The Everyday storefront focuses on a basic white T-shirt as an everyday wardrobe item. The brand direction is minimalist, with clear language and a simple product presentation.

The design direction includes:

- Neutral colors and clean layouts.
- Readable typography.
- Clear product imagery and product information.
- Straightforward navigation.
- Honest descriptions without fake reviews, false scarcity, or unsupported product claims.

The storefront research compares brands including UNIQLO, Hanes, Gildan, and American Apparel. The selected direction emphasizes a calm, product-focused presentation rather than a complicated shopping experience.

### Everyday User Stories

**Story 1 — Understand the store:** As a visitor, I want to understand what Everyday sells so I can decide whether the store offers what I am looking for.

**Story 2 — View the product:** As a potential customer, I want to see product information so I can understand the item before making a decision.

**Story 3 — Select a size and use the bag:** As a shopper, I want to select a size and add the product to a bag so I can review my selection.

**Story 4 — Explore styling suggestions:** As a visitor, I want optional styling suggestions so I can see how the product might fit into an outfit.

**Story 5 — Review checkout:** As a shopper, I want to move through a simulated checkout so I can understand the intended shopping flow.

### Everyday Observable Checks

- The homepage loads.
- Product images and descriptions display.
- Navigation links lead to the intended sections.
- Size selection and bag interactions respond as expected.
- Styling suggestions are optional and do not block access to product information.
- The simulated checkout communicates that it is a demonstration and does not imply a real purchase.

These checks should only be marked complete after they have been performed.
